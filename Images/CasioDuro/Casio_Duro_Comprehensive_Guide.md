# The Comprehensive Guide to the Casio Duro (MDV-106 & MDV-107)

![Casio Duro](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VatnosLedreme/CreativeMedia/refs/heads/main/Images/CasioDuro/casio-mdv106-review.webp "MDV-106")
Figure 01  - MDV-106 Caseback

The **Casio Duro** (specifically the MDV-106 and its successor, the MDV-107) is widely considered one of the greatest value-for-money watches in the world. Nicknamed "The Marlin" due to the iconic fish etched on its dial and case back, the Duro has earned a cult following among watch enthusiasts, scuba divers, and everyday wearers alike—including billionaire Bill Gates.

This document dives deep into the history, specifications, variations, and modding culture surrounding the legendary Casio Duro.

---

## 1. Introduction: The Legend of the Marlin

At a retail price frequently hovering between $40 and $70 USD, the Casio Duro offers features that usually cost hundreds of dollars more. It is a genuine ISO-compliant style dive watch, meaning it has the requisite water resistance, a unidirectional rotating bezel, and a screw-down crown necessary for actual scuba diving. 

It proves that one does not need to spend a fortune to acquire a durable, attractive, and highly functional timepiece.

## 3. Why is the Casio Duro So Popular?

### The "Bill Gates" Effect
Perhaps the most famous wearer of the Casio Duro is Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates. Frequently photographed wearing the classic black MDV-106-1AV, Gates' choice to wear a $50 watch despite being one of the wealthiest people on Earth has cemented the Duro's reputation as a "stealth wealth" or purely pragmatic icon.

### Unbeatable Value
Finding a watch with a full stainless steel case, a reliable quartz movement, 200m of water resistance, and a true screw-down crown for under $50 (on sale) is nearly impossible. It is the ultimate "beater" watch that you don't actually have to beat up.

### Versatility
With a 22mm lug width, the Duro is a strap monster. While the stock resin strap is stiff and utilitarian, the watch transforms completely when placed on a NATO strap, a Milanese mesh, a leather band, or a solid stainless steel Oyster bracelet.

## 4. Model Variations: MDV-106 vs. MDV-107

### The Marlin Era: MDV-106
The original and most sought-after version is the MDV-106. Its defining feature is the **Marlin logo** located on the lower half of the dial and engraved on the case back. 

* **MDV-106-1AV:** The classic black dial and black bezel.
* **MDV-106G-1A:** Black dial with gold accents on the bezel, hands, and indices.
* **MDV-106B-2A:** The "Batman" version (black dial, blue/black bezel).
* **MDV-106B-1A3V:** The "Kermit" or "Starbucks" version (black dial, green bezel).

### The Post-Marlin Era: MDV-107
In recent years, Casio’s licensing agreement for the Marlin logo expired. Consequently, Casio released the MDV-107. It is functionally and dimensionally identical to the MDV-106, but the Marlin logo has been removed from the dial and case back. 

* **MDV-107-1A1:** Classic Black.
* **MDV-107-1A2:** "Batman" (Black/Blue).
* **MDV-107-1A3:** "Pepsi" (Red/Blue bezel).

### The "Mini Duro": MDV-10D Series
Recognizing that 44mm is too large for many wrists, Casio released a smaller version (approx. 38.5mm). However, it sacrifices the 200m water resistance (downgraded to 50m) and the screw-down crown, making it more of a "desk diver" than a true dive watch.

## 5. The Modding Scene

Because of its low entry price, the Duro is a favorite among watch modders. Common modifications include:

1.  **Crystal Swaps:** Replacing the standard mineral crystal with a scratch-resistant **Sapphire Crystal** (often domed and with Anti-Reflective coating) is the most popular mod.
2.  **Bracelet Upgrades:** Swapping the resin strap for an aftermarket stainless steel Jubilee or Oyster bracelet fitted specifically for the Duro's curved lugs.
3.  **Hydro-Modding:** Filling the entire watch case with specialized silicone oil. This dramatically increases the water resistance (theoretically to thousands of meters) and makes the dial readable from extreme angles underwater.
4.  **Bezel Insert Swaps:** Changing the aluminum bezel insert to a ceramic one for better scratch resistance and a more premium look.

---

## 6. Pros and Cons

### Pros
* Exceptional value for money.
* True 200m dive watch capabilities.
* Handsome, classic diver aesthetics reminiscent of the Rolex Submariner.
* Extremely durable and reliable quartz movement.
* Looks incredible on aftermarket straps.

### Cons
* **Size:** At 44mm wide and 48mm lug-to-lug, it wears quite large and may not suit wrists under 6.5 inches.
* **Lume:** The luminous paint on the hands and markers fades very quickly in the dark.
* **Stock Strap:** The included resin strap is stiff, uncomfortable, and visually unappealing to many.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The Casio Duro is a masterpiece of accessible watchmaking. It strips away the pretense of luxury horology and delivers exactly what a tool watch should: reliability, durability, and legibility, all wrapped in a timeless design. Whether you are a seasoned collector with a box full of Rolexes and Omegas, or someone just looking for a tough watch for the weekend, the Duro deserves a spot in your collection.
